About

Working at the intersection of Genetics and Data

My research focuses on unravelling the molecular basis controlling flowering time in chickpea — the major domestication trait that defines the crop's adaptation to different agro-climatic conditions, and therefore a major determinant of its productivity.

Previously, I worked with Dr Juan Gil at the University of Córdoba as a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow under the Horizon 2020 programme, and with Dr Lisa J. Rowland at the USDA-ARS Genetic Improvement of Fruits and Vegetables Laboratory in Beltsville, MD (2012–2017). Between 2010 and 2012, I held a postdoctoral position funded through Spain's "Juan de la Cierva" programme.

"I look for ways to integrate bioinformatics with experimentation. I like data, and I like to extract biological value out of it."

I'm currently part of the Mejora Genética Vegetal research team, working on the identification and analysis of differentially expressed genes during plant development and biotic/abiotic stress. I'm an advocate of gene expression studies that comply with MIQE guidelines.

If you're interested in working together, feel free to reach out — I'm always glad to work with motivated, curious people.

Research focus

Three threads, one question

How does a plant know when to flower — and how can we use that knowledge to breed better crops, faster?

01 · Molecular Genetics

Flowering time in chickpea

Mapping how genetic variants combine to control adaptation, accelerating the introgression of new traits into breeding backgrounds.

02 · Big data, biological answers

Bioinformatics across species

Identifying differentially expressed genes during plant development and stress response by combining large datasets with hands-on experimentation.

03 · MIQE-compliant qPCR studies

Gene expression

Designing and running gene expression experiments that meet MIQE guidelines, so results stay reproducible and comparable across labs.
